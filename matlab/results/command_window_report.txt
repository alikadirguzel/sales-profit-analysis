-----------------------------------------------------------

There is statistically significant evidence that Urun is associated with differences in mean profit (classical ANOVA p = 9.490e-58, permutation ANOVA p = 9.999e-05).
The permutation test confirms that the observed between-group variation is unlikely to have arisen from random group assignment.
The largest observed sample-mean gap is Office Chair vs Pen Set (difference = 3791.17). A larger sample mean is not by itself a conclusion that one group "earns more"; that claim requires a significant overall test and, when applicable, a significant pairwise interval.
Urun explains approximately 27.06% of the variance in profit (eta^2 = 0.2706, omega^2 = 0.2619). Statistical significance is not the same as practical importance: with large n, small mean gaps can produce small p-values. Thresholds such as 0.01 / 0.06 / 0.14 are sometimes cited as small / medium / large but are context-dependent and are not applied here as automatic labels.
Kruskal-Wallis robustness check: H = 366.2244, p = 8.902e-72. This rank test is reported alongside ANOVA; it is not used to overwrite the permutation result.
Levene test rejected equal variances (p = 1.661e-77). Classical ANOVA p-values should be read with caution; permutation ANOVA remains the primary confirmatory test.
